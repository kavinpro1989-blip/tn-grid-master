## 👩‍🏫 Section A: 5 Questions from Teachers

### Q1 [M-T01]: "What made your team choose Tamil Nadu's energy grid for your project?"
* **Mithran's Answer:**
  > "We wanted to solve a real everyday mystery that every family in our school faces: Why do lights flicker or cut off right around 7 PM? When we researched it, we found out the issue isn't producing electricity—it's storing and balancing it. We chose Tamil Nadu because our state is India's clean energy leader, making the science real and local for everyone."

### Q2 [M-T02]: "What are the real-world clean energy statistics for Tamil Nadu?"
* **Mithran's Answer:**
  > "Tamil Nadu has the famous **Muppandal wind corridor** in Kanyakumari—one of the largest onshore wind farms on Earth—and the **Kamuthi Solar Park** in Ramanathapuram, which spans over 2,500 acres with 648 Megawatts capacity. On sunny, breezy afternoons, over **60%** of Tamil Nadu's electricity comes purely from clean renewables!"

### Q3 [M-T03]: "Why does the 'Evening Peak' cause such severe problems for the electricity board?"
* **Mithran's Answer:**
  > "Around 6:30 PM, two things happen simultaneously: the sun sets so solar generation drops to zero, while 80 million people return home and turn on heavy loads like air conditioners, lights, and water heaters. Because electricity cannot easily be stored in transmission wires, supply drops while demand skyrockets. If the gap isn't filled in seconds, transformers overheat and safety trips trigger blackouts."

### Q4 [M-T04]: "What does the term 'Cyber-Physical' mean in simple terms?"
* **Mithran's Answer:**
  > "It means bridging physical electrical hardware with digital computer software. The 'Physical' part is Faaris's real solar panel, relays, and spinning fan on our desk. The 'Cyber' part is Kavin's digital software simulation running on our screen. Together, they demonstrate how small local electronics interact with a statewide grid."

### Q5 [M-T05]: "How did your team divide the workload between the three of you?"
* **Mithran's Answer:**
  > "We divided it based on our individual strengths! Faaris built the physical circuit bench, wired the relays and solar panel, and wrote the Arduino C++ code. Kavin coded the digital twin simulation in JavaScript, modeled line losses, and calibrated the battery response. And I researched the energy data, organized our presentation, and connected the story to our school science concepts."

## 👨‍👩‍👧 Section B: 5 Questions from Parents

### Q6 [M-P01]: "Why do our home lights dim or the AC stop working during summer evenings?"
* **Mithran's Answer:**
  > "In summer evenings between 6:30 PM and 8:30 PM, almost every household across the city switches on their air conditioner at the same time. This sudden demand surge pulls massive electrical current through neighborhood transformers. When the grid gets overloaded, voltage drops—causing fans to slow down and lights to dim—until substations cut power to prevent equipment fires."

### Q7 [M-P02]: "If a family installs rooftop solar panels, why does their power still go off during a blackout?"
* **Mithran's Answer:**
  > "Most rooftop solar setups are 'grid-tied' without a battery. By government safety regulations, when street power cuts off, your solar inverter must automatically shut down. This is called 'anti-islanding' protection—it prevents your panels from sending dangerous electricity back into street cables where electricians are doing repair work. To have power during a blackout, you need an energy storage battery!"

### Q8 [M-P03]: "Is Tamil Nadu really that advanced in renewable energy, or do we mostly burn coal?"
* **Mithran's Answer:**
  > "During daytime afternoons, Tamil Nadu is extremely green—regularly generating over 60% of its power from sun and wind! However, at night when the sun is down and wind dies down, the state has to fire up coal thermal plants in North Chennai and Tuticorin. That's why our project focuses on large 485 MWh battery storage: to save daytime solar for nighttime use."

### Q9 [M-P04]: "Does using smart grids make electricity cheaper for everyday consumers?"
* **Mithran's Answer:**
  > "Yes! By capturing free midday solar power in batteries instead of turning on expensive emergency coal and diesel plants during peak hours, electricity boards save crores in fuel costs. Those savings help prevent power tariff hikes for households."

### Q10 [M-P05]: "What simple habits can families adopt to help the power grid during peak hours?"
* **Mithran's Answer:**
  > "Setting your air conditioner to 24°C instead of 18°C cuts its power draw by nearly 30%! Also, running heavy appliances like washing machines or water heaters during bright sunny mornings instead of peak evening hours significantly reduces strain on the grid."

## 🎒 Section C: 5 Questions from Students & Friends

### Q11 [M-S01]: "What is a 'Smart Grid'? How is it different from normal electricity?"
* **Mithran's Answer:**
  > "A normal grid only sends power in one direction—from big coal plants to your home switchboard—with no way to know if a wire is broken until people call and complain. A Smart Grid has sensors and computer brains (like our Arduino and digital twin) that detect voltage drops in milliseconds and automatically reroute battery power before lights even flicker!"

### Q12 [M-S02]: "Why did you guys pick this topic instead of building a robot or a game?"
* **Mithran's Answer:**
  > "Because we actually built both! We have a real working electronic circuit on our desk, and we have an interactive video-game-style simulation on our screen. Plus, power cuts happen to all of us, so solving a real problem that affects our daily lives felt exciting and meaningful."

### Q13 [M-S03]: "Can students build an energy monitoring setup like this at home?"
* **Mithran's Answer:**
  > "Yes! All the electronics Faaris used—the Arduino Uno, the 4.9V mini solar panel, and the 5V relay module—cost under ₹1,500 total and can be plugged together with simple jumper wires on a breadboard!"

### Q14 [M-S04]: "How did you three practice your speech without getting nervous?"
* **Mithran's Answer:**
  > "We did multiple 3-minute mock runs in front of each other! Our biggest trick was that each person only speaks for 60 to 75 seconds while pointing at the hardware or screen. When the audience looks at the cool project, the pressure is off our faces!"

### Q15 [M-S05]: "What happens if someone asks you a question that none of you know?"
* **Mithran's Answer:**
  > "We never panic! We simply say: 'That's a fantastic question! In this prototype we focused on the 230V household balance and battery response, but we'd love to research that and add it to our next upgrade!' It's always honest and confident."
